Know Your Hollow

What is a
blade hollow?

A skate blade is not flat on the bottom. The sharpening wheel cuts a shallow groove down the middle of it, and that groove leaves two edges standing proud on either side. The depth of that groove is the hollow, and it is the single biggest thing that decides how your skates feel. A deeper hollow bites harder and slows you down. A shallower hollow glides further and holds less. Most people are skating on whatever the last shop happened to have loaded, which is usually 1/2 inch.

Hollow Feel Usually right for
3/8" Deepest bite, slowest glide Light skaters, soft ice, players who feel like they are sliding out of every turn
1/2" The default everywhere Most minor hockey players. If nobody has ever asked you, this is what you have been getting
5/8" A little more glide Heavier or stronger skaters, players who feel like the ice is grabbing them
3/4" to 1" Speed over grip Big-body players, adult league, anyone chasing straight-line speed
1" and flatter Slide, not carve Goalies. Pushing across the crease is the job, not cutting into it

Do not know yours? Say so on the form and we will start you at 1/2 inch, write it down, and adjust from there. Once we find the one that feels right it goes on file and every sharpen after that is the same. That consistency is the part most shops cannot give you.

Questions

Skate sharpening questions.

The quick answers on pricing, hollows, turnaround, and where we are. Marshall's Home Team Sports sharpens hockey, goalie, and figure skates in Waterdown, Ontario.

Where can I get my skates sharpened in Waterdown? +

Marshall's Home Team Sports sharpens skates out of Waterdown, Ontario, and serves the surrounding area including Burlington, Flamborough, Dundas, and north Hamilton. There is a secure drop box at the Waterdown location for after-hours drop-off and pick-up, so you do not need to work around shop hours.

How much does skate sharpening cost? +

Flat pricing. A single sharpen is $12 per pair, a five-visit card is $50, and teams of ten pairs or more are $8 per pair. Goalie and figure skates are the same price as hockey skates. New skate profiling is $25, deep nick repair is $10, and same-day rush is $5.

How often should I sharpen my skates? +

A common rule of thumb is every 15 to 20 hours of ice time, which for most minor hockey players works out to somewhere between every four and every eight skates. It varies a lot though. Heavier skaters, harder ice, and stepping on rubber matting all dull an edge faster. The real test is whether your edges still bite on a hard stop. If you are sliding when you should be gripping, it is time.

Do you sharpen goalie and figure skates? +

Yes, both, and both get set up differently from player skates. Goalie blades get a flatter hollow so you can push across the crease instead of carving into it. Figure blades are ground with the toe pick fully preserved and the rocker left alone. Tell us your coach's spec and we will match it.

What hollow should I ask for? +

If nobody has ever asked you, you have almost certainly been skating on 1/2 inch, which is the default at most shops. That is a fine place to start. Lighter skaters and anyone who feels like they are sliding out of turns often prefer a deeper 3/8 inch. Heavier or stronger skaters usually want 5/8 inch or shallower for more glide. Goalies want 1 inch or flatter. See the hollow guide above for the full breakdown.

How long does it take? +

Most pairs dropped in the box are done the same day and you get a text when they are ready. Drop by noon and same-day is essentially guaranteed. Team bags of ten or more need a day's notice, especially on a tournament weekend.

Are you the same people who relace baseball gloves? +

Same shop, same person, same drop box. Marshall's Home Team Sports does baseball and softball glove relacing and baseball coaching as well. Gloves in the summer, skates in the winter. Same hands on both.
